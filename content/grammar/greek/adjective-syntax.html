Adjective Syntax

Goetchius, The Language of the New Testament §§60, 68

An adjective may be either in the attributive or the predicate position.

The following syntactical characteristics are important for adjectives:

  1. Concord/agreement: An adjective agrees with the noun it modifies in case, number, and gender (e.g. πιστός διάκονός)
  2. Position of adjectives:
    1. when an adjective modifies a noun with no article, it may either precede or follow (attributive)
    2. when an adjective modifies a noun with the definite article, it may have one of four positions
      Between the article and the noun (attributive): ὁ πιστός διάκονος
      Follow the noun, with repeated article (attributive): ὁ διάκονος ὁ πιστός
      Adjective precedes both noun and its article (first predicate): πιστός ὁ διάκονος
      Adjective has no article but follows the articular noun (second predicate): ὁ διάκονος πιστός
