Verb Types

Wallace, p. 39.

Verbs can be: transitive, intransitive, or equative.

Transitive: take a direct object and can typically be conformed into a passive construction (e.g., "the boy hit the ball").

Intransitive: does not take a direct object and cannot be transformed into a passive (e.g., "she came to the church").

Equative: they function like transitive verbs in typically having two substantives joined by a verb; but they also function like intransitive verbs in that they cannot be transformed (e.g., "John was a man").
